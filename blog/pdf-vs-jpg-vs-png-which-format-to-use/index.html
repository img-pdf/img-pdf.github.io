PDF vs JPG vs PNG: Which File Format Should You Use?

Choosing between PDF, JPG, and PNG shouldn't be guesswork. Each format was engineered for specific use cases with distinct strengths in text fidelity, photographic compression, and transparency.

1. Architectural Differences at a Glance

While people often refer to all three as "files," their underlying architectures are fundamentally distinct:

  • PDF (Portable Document Format): An ISO-standard container developed by Adobe. Rather than storing a single grid of pixels, a PDF encapsulates selectable text, font subsets, mathematical vector curves, forms, and embedded raster images across numbered physical pages.
  • JPG / JPEG (Joint Photographic Experts Group): A raster bitmap format designed specifically for continuous-tone photography. It uses lossy compression by discarding color data that the human visual system is least sensitive to.
  • PNG (Portable Network Graphics): A raster format engineered to replace GIF. It uses lossless DEFLATE compression and supports 8-bit alpha channel transparency with crystal-clear edges.

3. When You Must Use PDF

PDF is mandatory whenever document structure, page bounds, and text integrity must remain inviolate regardless of the screen or printer rendering it:

  • Employment Resumes & Portfolios: Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) read true text streams in PDFs to parse skills and experience. Saving a resume as a JPG makes your text invisible to search crawlers.
  • Legal & Business Contracts: Retaining page numbers, digital signatures, and precise margins across multi-page agreements.
  • Official Submissions: Government immigration, visa, and grant portals almost universally reject loose image files in favor of PDF.

4. When JPG is the Superior Choice

JPG remains undefeated for real-world photographs and colorful continuous-tone artwork:

  • Camera shots with millions of colors and natural lighting gradients.
  • E-commerce product photos where fast thumbnail loading is critical.
  • Email newsletter headers where file size must stay under 100KB.

5. When PNG is Essential

PNG is the undisputed champion for high-contrast graphics, crisp geometric lines, and transparent assets:

  • Company logos and watermarks placed over variable website backgrounds.
  • Software screenshots, UI mockups, and infographic diagrams containing fine text.
  • Scanned signatures with the white paper background erased.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

Can a JPG contain multiple pages?
No. The JPEG standard only defines a single two-dimensional raster image. To package multiple pages together, you must convert your JPG files into a PDF document.
Does converting PNG to JPG ruin transparency?
Yes. Because JPG cannot encode alpha transparency, transparent areas are permanently filled with solid white (or black) during conversion.
What is WebP and how does it fit in?
WebP is a modern image format combining the best attributes of JPG and PNG: it supports photographic compression like JPG, transparency like PNG, and is typically 25% smaller.
