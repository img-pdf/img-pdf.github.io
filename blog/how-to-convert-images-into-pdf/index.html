From official passport photos and expense receipts to smartphone book scans, converting loose images into a clean, uniform PDF is the standard requirement for professional sharing. Here is how to do it instantly without losing resolution.

1. Why Convert Images to PDF Instead of Sending JPEGs?

Sending standalone photo attachments (like IMG_4901.jpg) often causes rendering problems: messaging apps compress them aggressively, email services display them in erratic thumbnail grids, and print dimensions become unpredictable. Packing images into a PDF provides three major advantages:

  • Fixed Physical Dimensions: PDFs specify exact real-world page dimensions (such as standard A4 or US Letter), ensuring the recipient prints your documents at true 100% scale without edge clipping.
  • Universal Compatibility: Every smartphone, tablet, desktop operating system, and web browser has a built-in PDF viewer.
  • Multi-Page Continuity: You can bundle 10 photos of a multi-page contract into a single chronological file rather than emailing 10 separate attachments.

2. Supported Formats: JPG, PNG, and Modern WebP

Different image formats serve different purposes:

  • JPEG / JPG: Best for real-world photographic scans, ID cards, and receipts. The lossy DCT compression keeps document sizes remarkably small.
  • PNG: Best for screenshots, line drawings, charts, and digital signatures where crisp high-contrast pixel boundaries and alpha transparency are required.
  • WebP: Google's modern format providing 25% to 34% smaller file sizes than comparable JPEGs. ImgPDF decodes WebP natively using the browser graphics pipeline.

4. Page Layout: A4 vs US Letter and Margin Setup

When converting photos of documents, setting proper margins prevents your content from getting cut off during physical printing:

  • Small Margin (10mm / 0.5 in): Recommended for forms, receipts, and scans where you want maximum printable area while preserving standard desktop printer grab margins.
  • Zero Margin (Full Bleed): Ideal for presentation slides, full-page artwork, and high-resolution photo portfolios.
  • Auto-Orientation: The tool automatically checks whether each image is portrait or landscape and matches the PDF page orientation accordingly, avoiding unwanted sideways rotation.

5. Balancing File Size vs Visual Clarity

Modern smartphone cameras shoot 12MP to 48MP photos, resulting in 5MB to 15MB per picture. A 5-page PDF made from raw camera photos could easily reach 50MB—far above typical 5MB email limits.
