Converting Images to PDF: Margins, Page Scaling & Print Setup

IP
ImgPDF Technical Team
Published: 2026 · 5 min read · Document Publishing

Packing photographs, scanned receipts, passport forms, and classroom assignments into a standardized PDF is a universal necessity. Unlike raw image files which open with different zoom levels and orientation bugs across devices, a well-formed PDF guarantees predictable page sequencing, standardized dimensions, and reliable printing.

1. Understanding the PDF Coordinate System

In standard PDF architecture, page dimensions and vector elements are measured in Typographic Points (PostScript points), defined exactly as 1/72 of an inch (approximately 0.3528 mm). This means standard physical sheet sizes convert to points as follows:

Page Format Physical Dimensions PDF Point Dimensions [Width × Height] Typical Regional Use
ISO A4 210 × 297 mm 595.28 × 841.89 pt International Standard (Europe, Asia, South America)
US Letter 8.5 × 11 inches 612.00 × 792.00 pt United States, Canada, Mexico
Fit to Image Variable Matches Image Native Pixel Dimensions Digital viewing, art portfolios, screenshots

2. Fitting Images Without Aspect Distortion

When placing an arbitrary image onto a standardized page (like A4), the image rarely shares the exact 1:1.414 aspect ratio of the sheet. Encoders must compute the aspect ratio scaling factor:

scaleRatio = Math.min(availableWidth / imageWidth, availableHeight / imageHeight);
renderedWidth = imageWidth × scaleRatio;
renderedHeight = imageHeight × scaleRatio;

By taking the minimum of the horizontal and vertical ratios, the image scales to fit completely within the printable boundaries without being cropped or distorted. Centering offsets are then calculated to position the graphic evenly between the margins.

3. Auto-Orientation: Portrait vs. Landscape

A frequent annoyance when combining mobile camera photos is mixed orientations—some pages are vertical portrait receipts while others are horizontal landscape forms. A robust converter must inspect each image's natural dimensions:

  • If width > height, the page dynamically flips its bounding box to Landscape (e.g. 841.89 × 595.28 pt for A4).
  • If height > width, the page remains in Portrait.

4. Pre-Compression to Prevent Multi-Hundred-Megabyte PDFs

A single 48-megapixel mobile snapshot can be 15 MB in size. Stacking 10 such raw images into a PDF creates a 150 MB document that cannot be attached to email (standard 25 MB email limits).
